Learn the world, sample the person

The twin shows the range of lives one person could lead over the next ten years, given how they tend to decide. It does not optimise. Two learned parts make that possible: a world model that learns from the ledger how a month unfolds, and a persona policy that chooses the way this person would, at random and with memory of their own path.

The loop

One person, thousands of lives

Each month the persona policy samples a choice from the person's traits and their own history so far, and the world model samples what happens next. One path is 120 months; the explorer runs 2,000 paths and summarises the spread.

World model T(s′ | s, a)

Learn what the data can teach, state the rest

The ledger shows 1,200 people for 24 months, but nobody in it changes mode, flat or spending. So the network learns how a month unfolds for a given state, and an action works by moving the person into a state other people already occupy. That is exactly how the synthetic data was generated; with real data it becomes an assumption to test.

Why not one network for the whole step? Bookkeeping is exact, so a network could only add error to it. And since nobody switches in the data, a network trained on whole steps would learn that actions do nothing.

One step runs in this order: apply the action (mode and rent redraws, transfers) → draw events and the economy → draw the month's flow from the network → update the books.

Flow network

A small network whose output is a distribution

Inputs
  • work mode, plus a self-employed flag
  • net_income and rent (log)
  • household and housing tier
  • city (small embedding) and age
  • calendar month (12)

Left out on purpose: the calendar year (drift enters through net_income and rent), and lifestyle and economy, which never vary in the data and are applied afterwards as prior adjustments.

Output and training
  • a mixture of 5 Gaussians over next month's net flow, so bonuses, bills and fees fit as separate bumps
  • MLP with 2 × 64 units; negative log-likelihood; AdamW, weight decay, early stopping
  • 5 folds split by person, so nobody is in both training and test
  • 5 seeds: each path draws one member, so model uncertainty shows in the spread, and members that disagree flag states the data never covered

Acceptance tests before it replaces the hand-calibrated simulator

  1. At least as good on held-out people: log-likelihood and CRPS no worse than the hand-calibrated simulator.
  2. Trained on 2025 only, its 2026 intervals cover about 50% and 90% of end-of-year cash (baseline: 51% and 89%).
  3. Rolled 12 months forward from December 2025, about 61 profiles go below zero in 2026 (data: 61; baseline: 61.2).
  4. Simulated months show the patterns of the data: a December bonus for about 56% of employees, bills in 37% of Februaries and Augusts, noise of about CHF 200 + 5% of pay, no carry-over between months, and bills unrelated to pay.
Persona policy π(a | s, h, z)

Choose like this person, not optimally

The policy gives a probability for every option, conditioned on the state, the person's own history h and their traits z. It is always sampled, never maximised, so two people with the same balances can choose differently, and so can the same person on two paths.

Traits z

Risk tolerance, frugality, career drive, wish for free time, housing aspiration, inertia, impatience and goal focus, plus a temperature for how random choices are.

Set from the profile (age, mode, household, goal type: an "Education flexibility" goal raises the pull toward a master's), a short questionnaire, and a spread around the answers.

History h

Months in the current mode, months since the last move, recent shocks (job loss, a big bill), cash trend, goal gap against time left, and past switches.

The neural version reads the monthly path with a small GRU (32 units) instead.

Output

Four heads in order: status (5) → housing (3) → spending (3) → assets (3), each masked to valid options.

Status and housing are considered at a yearly review or right after an event; spending and assets every month.

The person can state intentions too, each with a probability: "start a master's in September 2026, 60%" or "move somewhere cheaper if cash falls below two months". Each adds to the logit of the matching option.

StageWhatWhy
1Rule persona (MVP)Trait-weighted rules plus inertia, turned into probabilities with the person's temperatureWorks on day one and is easy to explain
2Neural personaOne network for all personas, cloned from rule-persona paths plus LLM persona demonstrations: an LLM plays sampled personas at decision points and returns a probability per optionRemembers history, fast enough for 2,000 paths, richer than rules

The data holds no decisions, so behaviour cannot be checked against it. Check instead that yearly action rates look plausible against base rates the team sources, and show how results move when each trait changes.

Explorer

What one person gets to see

  1. Four sources of spread on every path: traits drawn from the person's range, one ensemble member, the policy's choices, and the world's luck (flows, events, markets).
  2. Fan charts of cash and net worth, and the odds of a shortfall or of meeting each goal, year by year.
  3. The five most common life stories (clusters of yearly work mode, housing and household), each with its probability and typical outcome.
  4. Turning points: the decisions and events that most separate good from bad outcomes.
  5. What-ifs such as "start the master's now" on the same random numbers, so differences come from the change alone.
  6. Where the spread comes from: choices, luck or model uncertainty, found by fixing one source at a time.

2,000 paths × 120 months run batched in PyTorch (2.11, already installed here) in seconds.

Risks

What could go wrong, and the answer

RiskAnswer
Only 1,200 peopleSmall network, ensembles, folds by person, and the baseline as a floor
Nobody switches in the dataAction effects come only from comparing people; say so, and flag states where the ensemble disagrees
Ten years from two years of dataDrift through net_income and rent, never the calendar; priors for everything else
Errors compound over 120 monthsExact bookkeeping, the 12-month rollout test, and pattern tests on simulated paths
Personas are assumptionsVisible traits, base-rate checks, and sensitivity to each trait
LLM costOnly at decision points, for training data or a few narrated paths; never inside the 2,000-path loop
