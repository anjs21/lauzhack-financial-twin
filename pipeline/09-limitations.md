# 9. Limitations, and what would make the twin more reliable

**In short.** The parts that can be tested against the data pass. The weak points are the ones the
data cannot reach: what happens after a change of situation, anything beyond two years, and how
people really decide. Below, each weakness comes with the check or fix that would address it.

## The data

| Limitation | Consequence | What would help |
|---|---|---|
| The data is synthetic, generated by simple rules close to our model's structure | Fitting it well is expected, and proves less than it would on real ledgers (the hand-calibrated simulator separates who runs short perfectly, AUC 1.0) | Real or more realistic panel data |
| Two years of history, ten-year paths | Years 3 to 10 rest on constant drift and on the priors | A sensitivity sweep of every prior (below) |
| Nobody changes situation in the data | Pay and rent after a change come from comparing people (rank-preserving redraws). That is exact for this data and untested for real people. | Panel data with job changes and moves |
| Education, grades and gross pay are missing | Education is drawn from an assumed prior per life | Let users state them (already possible) and show how much they matter |

## The transition function

| Limitation | Evidence | What would help |
|---|---|---|
| End-2026 forecasts lean slightly low | Observed cash in the top tenth of the forecast 158 times, bottom tenth 101 (expected 120) | Recalibrate the offset or the variance; check by work status |
| The network is slightly worse than the baseline for the self-employed and the unemployed | CRPS 1,004 vs 989 and 226 vs 220 | More capacity or a separate noise term for the self-employed |
| T2 and T3 use drift fitted on both years | The replay without drift gives the same flow CRPS, so the effect is small | Fit the drift on 2025 only inside the backtest |
| Lifestyle, the economy regime and children never vary in the data | They enter as stated adjustments, not learned effects | Nothing in this data can fix it; keep them visible as assumptions |
| Every number in `priors.yaml` is an assumption | Job loss, promotion, family events, returns and credit drive the ten-year spread | **A sensitivity sweep:** change each value by ±50% and report how goal odds move, by work status |

## The markets

| Limitation | Evidence | What would help |
|---|---|---|
| Spells without work are too short | 3.5 months vs 6.5 (BFS) | Separate knobs for openings and hiring selectivity |
| Bachelor's-only graduates are unemployed too often | 26% vs 4.9% (BFS figure for applied-science bachelor's) | Separate university and applied-science bachelor's |
| Master's to PhD target is assumed | 15%, no verified BFS figure | Find the figure |
| Error grows outside the training sizes | 3.0 points for job offers at 5,000 people | Add 5,000-person societies to training |
| Validated against a simulation, not individuals | The society is calibrated to aggregates | Compare with published transition rates by degree and age |

## The persona

| Limitation | Evidence | What would help |
|---|---|---|
| Behaviour cannot be checked against decisions | The data has none | Compare yearly rates with Swiss base rates; a small user study |
| Flat changes are rare | 1.6% a year vs the Swiss moving rate of 9.3%; the twin only models moves to another size of flat | Model same-size moves, or compare with the right statistic |
| Lifestyle changes are a little frequent | 10.9% a year vs an assumed 5–10% | Tune the lifestyle scores |
| Trait defaults are hand-written rules | e.g. risk tolerance from the share of savings invested | Calibrate against survey data on financial attitudes |
| The neural clone and foresight are older than the markets | Trained on the previous dynamics; the gpt-4o-mini labels describe an older decision clock | Re-run `llm-demos`, `clone --llm` and `foresight` |
| The January 2026 Qwen proxy rests on 60 labels | Held-out KL on 9 profiles | More labels before relying on it |

## The planner agent

| Limitation | What would help |
|---|---|
| Plan quality depends on the LLM; tested end to end only with Qwen3-8B | Run the same wishes with a larger model and compare plans |
| A small model sometimes does not sign within 4 rounds | Shown as "not signed"; a larger model should need fewer rounds |
| Plans live in memory for an hour in the API | Fine for a demo; persistent storage would need consent and privacy rules |

## An open team decision

**The student credit limit.** A student on CHF 1,200 a month with about CHF 2,700 of rent and living
costs crosses a limit of 3 × pay (CHF 5,000 floor) in the first year whatever they choose, and the
breach fails every goal. Real students have family support and student loans. We need to decide
whether to raise the limit for students or add a support term.

## Checks we would run next

1. **A sweep over the priors:** ±50% on each value in `priors.yaml`, goal odds and shortfall odds by
   work status, as a tornado chart.
2. **Monte Carlo error:** report it next to every probability. At 2,000 lives the standard error of
   a probability is at most 1.1 points.
3. **Where the ensemble disagrees:** flag states where the five flow-network members disagree, as a
   warning that the person is outside what the data covered.
4. **Calibration by group:** repeat the T2 band check by work status and by pay fifth.
5. **Population check over ten years:** simulate all 1,200 profiles and compare the twin's
   unemployment rate, moving rate and master's uptake with the Swiss figures.
6. **Retrain** the clone and foresight on the current dynamics, then re-run their comparisons.
