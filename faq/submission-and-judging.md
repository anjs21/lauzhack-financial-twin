# FAQ — Submission, Demo, and Judging

**LauzHack 2026 | Participant guidance**

> **Important:** The repository's evaluation rubric is a **proposal**, not binding event policy until approved by organizers. Official rules and deadlines take precedence.

### 1. What should our demo show?
A fictional profile, a meaningful decision, a baseline/alternative comparison, explicit assumptions, and an understandable explanation.

### 2. Is a deployed website mandatory?
Not specified by this FAQ. A working local or hosted prototype may be appropriate; confirm official submission requirements.

### 3. Must we submit code?
Check the official event rules. A repository with setup instructions and reproducible examples is recommended.

### 4. Do we need a presentation?
Confirm with organizers. A short explanation of the problem, model, demo, and limitations is useful.

### 5. How long is the demo?
To be confirmed by organizers.

### 6. What is the submission deadline?
To be confirmed by organizers.

### 7. What is the team-size limit?
To be confirmed by organizers.

### 8. What is the proposed evaluation structure?
The draft `evaluation/judging-rubric.md` proposes:
- Financial modelling and correctness: 25 points
- Scenario and counterfactual exploration: 20 points
- Technical execution and validation: 15 points
- User experience and communication: 15 points
- Innovation and conceptual depth: 15 points
- Responsible design and transparency: 10 points

These weights are **not official** until approved.

### 9. Will using an LLM earn extra points?
Not by itself. Evidence of useful, accurate capability matters more than the name of a tool.

### 10. Are games eligible?
Yes as an approach, subject to official event rules. Demonstrate that gameplay is linked to financial calculations.

### 11. Is forecasting accuracy a judging criterion?
Not in the proposed rubric. The challenge focuses on conditional exploration, mathematical consistency, and explainability.

### 12. Should we disclose assumptions?
Yes. A scenario without explicit assumptions is difficult to evaluate.

### 13. How can we demonstrate technical rigor?
Show one or more reproducible tests, model checks, constraints, or benchmark comparisons.

### 14. What if our prototype is incomplete?
Explain what works, what does not, and how the remaining limitations affect the results.

### 15. Where is the judging scorecard?
See `evaluation/judge-scorecard.md`. It is a draft for organizer approval.

### 16. Can we use third-party code and data?
Follow licenses and event rules. Attribute sources and avoid publishing restricted materials.
