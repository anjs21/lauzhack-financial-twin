# Financial Concepts for the AI Financial Twin

## 1. Purpose

This document introduces financial concepts that may help participants develop an AI Financial Twin.

The challenge is to explore how life decisions could affect a person's financial situation over time.

Participants are free to choose their modelling approach and level of complexity.

All numerical examples are illustrative and do not constitute financial advice.

## 2. Income

Income represents money received over a period.

Examples include:

- Employment salary
- Part-time work
- Freelance income
- Investment income
- Scholarships or grants

It is important to distinguish gross income from net disposable income.

Gross income is the amount before taxes and deductions. Net income reflects relevant deductions and is generally more useful for personal cash-flow modelling.

Income may change over time due to career progression, employment changes, education or relocation.

## 3. Expenses

Expenses represent money spent.

Common categories include:

- Housing and utilities
- Food and transportation
- Education and tuition
- Healthcare and insurance
- Taxes and mandatory contributions
- Travel and discretionary spending
- Debt repayments

Some expenses are recurring, while others are one-time costs.

For example, moving abroad may involve an initial relocation cost followed by a different monthly cost of living.

## 4. Cash Flow

Cash flow measures money entering and leaving a person's finances over a defined period.

A simplified calculation is:

**Net cash flow = Cash inflows − Cash outflows**

Example:

Monthly net income: CHF 4,500

Monthly expenses: CHF 3,200

Monthly net cash flow: CHF 1,300

Positive cash flow can increase savings or support investments.

Negative cash flow may require drawing on savings, borrowing or changing spending.

## 5. Savings and Liquidity

Savings represent money accumulated over time.

Liquidity refers to the availability of funds to meet financial obligations.

A simplified savings model is:

**Closing savings = Opening savings + Net cash flow + Interest earned**

Example:

Opening savings: CHF 8,000

Monthly net cash flow: CHF 500

After 12 months, assuming no interest or other changes:

Closing savings = CHF 14,000

A Financial Twin may help users understand how a decision affects their available savings buffer.

## 6. Investments and Compounding

Investments may generate positive or negative returns.

A simplified investment model is:

**Future value = Present value × (1 + r)^n**

Where:

- r = assumed return per period
- n = number of periods

Example:

CHF 10,000 invested at an illustrative annual return of 4% for five years would grow to approximately CHF 12,167, assuming annual compounding, no contributions, fees or taxes.

Investment returns are uncertain. A fixed return is a modelling assumption, not a guaranteed outcome.

## 7. Inflation

Inflation describes changes in the general price level over time.

A simplified inflation-adjustment formula is:

**Future cost = Current cost × (1 + inflation rate)^n**

For example, an expense of CHF 1,000 today would be approximately CHF 1,104 after five years under an assumed constant annual inflation rate of 2%.

Inflation assumptions can affect living expenses, purchasing power and long-term financial plans.

## 8. Debt and Borrowing

Borrowing creates an obligation to repay principal and potentially interest.

Important concepts include:

- Loan principal
- Interest rate
- Repayment schedule
- Monthly payment
- Outstanding balance
- Debt affordability

A home-purchase scenario may require assumptions about down payments, mortgage interest, amortization and ongoing property costs.

## 9. Financial Goals

Financial goals may include:

- Completing a degree
- Building an emergency fund
- Buying a home
- Starting a business
- Moving abroad
- Achieving financial independence
- Planning retirement

Different goals may compete for the same financial resources.

A Financial Twin should help make those trade-offs understandable.

## 10. Risk and Uncertainty

Financial outcomes depend on assumptions that may not materialize.

Sources of uncertainty include:

- Employment and salary changes
- Investment performance
- Inflation
- Interest rates
- Unexpected expenses
- Changes in personal circumstances

Possible approaches include sensitivity analysis, alternative scenarios, probabilistic modelling and stress testing.

A useful Financial Twin should distinguish assumptions, calculated outcomes and uncertainty.

## 11. The Alexia Example

Alexia is a fictional 20-year-old student living in Lausanne.

Her initial savings buffer is CHF 8,000.

She is considering three possible paths:

1. Start working
2. Pursue a master's degree
3. Move abroad

Each option could affect her income, education costs, living expenses and savings differently.

Participants may use these choices as inspiration or create entirely different financial scenarios.

**Key takeaway:** Financial modelling is about understanding relationships, assumptions and trade-offs — not predicting the future with certainty.
