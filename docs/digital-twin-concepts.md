# Understanding Digital Twins

## 1. What Is a Digital Twin?

A digital twin is a digital representation of a physical entity or system, connected to relevant information about that entity or system.

Depending on its design, a digital twin may support monitoring, modelling, simulation, analysis, and decision-making.

Unlike a static digital model, a digital twin is generally associated with an ongoing relationship between a real-world system and its digital representation.

## 2. Historical Foundations

The development of digital twins builds on decades of simulation, modelling, and product lifecycle management.

### 1970 — Apollo 13

NASA used spacecraft simulators and engineering models to help develop and test emergency procedures during the Apollo 13 mission.

Apollo 13 is an important historical example of simulation, but it was not a digital twin in the modern technical sense.

### 2002 — Michael Grieves

Michael Grieves introduced a conceptual model linking physical products with virtual representations in product lifecycle management.

This became an important foundation of modern digital-twin thinking.

### 2010 — NASA Roadmapping

The term *digital twin* emerged in NASA technology roadmapping associated with John Vickers.

### 2012 — Digital Twin Paradigm

NASA researchers published a digital-twin framework involving physical models, operational information, and simulation.

### 2016 — Industry Recognition

Gartner identified digital twins as an emerging technology trend for 2017.

## 3. Digital Twins Across Industries

**Manufacturing:** Virtual factories can help engineers investigate production layouts and processes before physical implementation.

**Healthcare:** Patient-specific computational heart models can support research into treatment planning for certain cardiac conditions.

**Smart Cities:** Digital models can help planners investigate infrastructure, mobility, and urban-development scenarios.

**Energy:** Models of wind turbines and other assets can support performance analysis and maintenance planning.

**Maritime Operations:** Simulation can support the analysis of vessel movements, logistics, and port operations.

## 4. Four Useful Building Blocks

### Data — What do we know today?

Information describing the system and its current state.

### Model — How does the system behave?

Mathematical, statistical, computational, or other representations of relevant relationships.

### Scenarios — What could happen if conditions change?

Alternative assumptions or decisions are explored through the model.

### Explain — What do the outcomes mean?

Results are interpreted and communicated with their assumptions and limitations.

These four building blocks provide a useful framework for the challenge, rather than a universal technical definition of every digital twin.

## 5. Applying the Concept to Personal Finance

A Financial Twin could represent an individual's financial situation, including income, expenses, savings, investments, obligations, goals, and preferences.

It could then help explore questions such as:

- What if I start working instead of pursuing a master's degree?
- What if I move abroad?
- What if I buy a home?
- What if I retire earlier?
- What happens under different market conditions?

Financial futures are inherently uncertain. A Financial Twin should therefore communicate plausible outcomes under stated assumptions rather than claim to predict an individual's future.
